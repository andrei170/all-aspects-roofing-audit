Finding 1 — 402 Pages, One Page
Pages in your sitemap 402 Structure 11 services × ~35 towns Words per page ~1,400 We compared four of your town pages word by word: Bath vs Frome 4,995 of 5,017 unique words shared Bath vs Bristol 5,001 of 5,017 shared Bath vs Salisbury 5,003 of 5,017 shared The handful of words that differ are web addresses inside the page code, not readable content.
And the page author gives it away

The WordPress author on those pages is called template2023. You can see it yourself — it is in the page code on every one of them.

Check it in two minutes: open your Bath page and your Frome page side by side and read the first three paragraphs of each. They are identical apart from the town name.

Why it matters: Google is explicit that pages created mainly for search engines, with little unique value, may be treated as spam. Four hundred copies of one page is the textbook example. At best they do nothing; at worst they hold back the pages that would otherwise rank.

We are not telling you the site is worthless — the underlying content is decent and the structure is sensible. We are telling you it was mass-produced, and that you should know that before you spend anything else.

Finding 2 — You Sell Two Markets And Only One Of Them Has Any Pages
Your homepage heading: "Roofing Company in Frome, Somerset & London" Your meta description: "...in Frome, Somerset & London, 24/7" Your page keywords: "roof repairs frome, roofing london" Your address in the site code: streetAddress "St Mary's Cres, Stanwell" (TW19 — near Heathrow) postalCode "TW19 7HU" addressLocality "Frome" addressRegion "Somerset" Companies House registered office: 54a Church Road, Ashford, Middlesex, TW15 2TS Of your 402 pages, how many target London or Middlesex: zero
This is not the error we first thought it was — it is a bigger opportunity

You tell visitors you cover Frome, Somerset and London. Your registered office is in Middlesex and the address in your site code is a Stanwell postcode. So the London side of the business is real.

But every one of your 402 pages targets Somerset and Wiltshire. Bath, Bristol, Wells, Warminster, Trowbridge, Yeovil. Not one page for anywhere in London, Middlesex or Surrey — not Staines, not Ashford, not Hounslow, not Twickenham, not anywhere inside the M25.

Half of what you say you sell has no pages behind it at all.

And there is one genuine error inside the address, worth ten minutes

Your structured data pairs a Stanwell street and a TW19 postcode with the locality "Frome" and the region "Somerset". Those describe two different places roughly a hundred miles apart, in a single address block.

Structured data is one of the strongest signals Google uses to decide where a business is. Giving it a self-contradicting address is worse than giving it either one on its own.

The fix depends on how you actually trade. If Frome is the main base, the postcode should be a Frome one. If there are genuinely two locations, they should be two separate address entries — and the London one deserves its own pages.

Check it yourself: view the source of your homepage and search for postalCode.

Finding 3 — Two Sitemaps, One Of Them Empty
/sitemap.xml plugin-generated → 402 pages listed /wp-sitemap.xml WordPress built-in → completely empty
Small, but it is a symptom

Two sitemap systems are running at once. The plugin one works; the WordPress one publishes an empty file.

It will not be costing you much, but it is the kind of thing left behind when a site is built quickly and never tidied.

Finding 4 — No Enquiry Form On Your Homepage, And No Meta Pixel
Homepage: Tappable phone numbers 4 Enquiry form none found (WPForms is installed on the site) Tracking: Google tag GT-KD2WJK84 present Meta pixel not found Google Tag Manager not found Call tracking not found
Two gaps, one of which matters more given what you are spending

Your phone numbers are properly linked on every page and that is right for roofing — most repair enquiries are a call. But there is no form on the homepage, so anybody who would rather type than talk has nowhere to go. The form software is already installed.

And there is no Meta pixel anywhere. If any part of that £10,000 is going to Facebook or Instagram, none of it is being measured and none of that audience is being kept for retargeting.

Without call tracking either, you cannot currently tell which of your channels produced which job — which is a difficult position to be in at that level of spend.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the address block so the postcode and the locality describe the same place — or split it into two proper locations if you trade from both. Ten minutes, free.
  • Decide whether London is real. You advertise it on every page and have built nothing for it. If it is real, that is the largest untapped thing you own; if it is not, take it off the site.
  • Put call tracking on. At roughly £10,000 of spend, not knowing which channel produced which job is the most expensive gap you have.
  • Add the form to the homepage. The software is already installed.
  • Decide what to do about the 402 pages. Our view is to keep and properly rewrite the towns you actually work in, and remove the rest — but that is a real decision and we would want your job data before recommending which towns stay.
  • Add the Meta pixel if any of the spend is going to Facebook or Instagram.
  • Then, and only then, look at the advertising itself. There is no point optimising spend into a site that is telling Google it is in Surrey.
